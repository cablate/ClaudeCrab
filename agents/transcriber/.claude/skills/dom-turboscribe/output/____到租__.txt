How does Netflix make money? If your answer is to sell movies or TV series, then you only see the surface. What really makes Netflix a hard copy machine is not that you watch a movie, but that you can't stop at all. Netflix has an internal data, users watch it for 32 hours on average per month.

But the point is, 70% of the profits of Netflix come from those who click on the next episode and play it automatically. In other words, what Netflix sells is not content, it is an entrance that you can never finish watching. You go to watch those automatic play, skip the credits, personalized recommendations, all to extend your stay time, because as long as you are still brushing, you will not cancel your subscription.

This is the first iron law of wealth expansion, do not hold the result of a single transaction, but lock the entrance of a continuous transaction. In traditional business, every transaction is like throwing a dice, win one and lose one. But the real masters don't play single play from the beginning.

What they build is a system that makes others unable to stop, just like a gym that sells annual cards. They know you will only go three times, but what they want is the action that you pay for every year. The e-commerce platform pushes membership, even if you only buy twice a year, but the monthly fee is deducted automatically.

The logic behind this is called behavior kidnapping. When your business can make users have habitual actions, even if the single profit is zero, you have already won. But the worse is yet to come, because the people who really set the rules never play the game.

Starbucks is one of the companies that knows leverage the most in the world. The headquarters never cooks coffee in person, but can control the income of 30,000 stores worldwide. The core secret of wealth expansion is hidden in it.

There are only three types of leverage income, human power, capital and code. Human leverage is the most primitive, you hire ten people, in theory, you can double the output. But the problem is that people will be tired, they will skip work, they will ask for a raise.

What's more fatal is the physical limit of human leverage management. A person can effectively manage no more than seven subordinates, which means that your organization will increase by a layer and the cost of communication will rise exponentially. Starbucks has long seen through this dead end.

Instead of letting the headquarters directly manage 300,000 employees, it is better to establish a regional management system. Ten stores are managed by each regional manager, and 15 employees are managed by each store manager. This structure makes Starbucks ten times the efficiency of human leverage, but it still can't get rid of a fate.

Your income growth will never catch up with your human cost growth. Capital leverage is more advanced. You invest 1 million yuan to open a store, earn 200,000 yuan a year, and the return rate is 20%.

But if you use this 1 million yuan as a guarantee to raise 5 million loans and open five stores at the same time, earn 1 million yuan a year, the return rate directly becomes 100%. This is why real estate developers and chain enterprises are crazily adding leverage. Because capital leverage can make your expansion speed jump from the arithmetic level to the geometric level.

But there is a problem here. Capital leverage has a ceiling. When your debt ratio exceeds 70%, any fluctuation in the economic cycle can make you collapse instantly.

In 2008, the financial crisis, many companies with leverage went bankrupt overnight because they regarded capital leverage as a mobile phone. But the most cruel thing is code leverage, which is system leverage. If you write a code on WeChat, it can serve 100 million users, and the editing cost is almost zero.

Starbucks builds a transaction system, handles tens of millions of orders every day, and doesn't need to add a customer service. This is why Internet companies' valuation can reach hundreds of billions, because their income growth is not limited by human resources and capital. Starbucks' ultimate leverage is not how many employees it employs, or how much capital it has, but it standardizes and digitalizes the coffee production process, the store selection model, and the member operating system.

Any partner can copy its success as long as they join Starbucks. This means that Starbucks' expansion speed no longer relies on talent reserve, nor is it limited by capital, but depends on the system's copying efficiency. Now you can think about which leverage you use in your business.

If you still rely on your own time and energy to make money, you will never escape the trap of linear growth. If you use human resources, your ceiling is the management environment. If you use capital, your risk is debt crisis.

But if you can standardize business, process it, and let the system operate by itself, you can make money while sleeping. But there is a fatal mistake here. Everyone wants to copy successfully, but they copy the wrong target.

They think that copying means opening more stores, hiring more people, and making more money. But they don't know that the real copy is the bottom-level system that allows business to operate automatically. Ants are one of the most successful species on Earth.

The information speed tells it where to go, and it goes where it goes. This is the bottom-level logic of systematic expansion. The copy system is more cruel than the copyist.
